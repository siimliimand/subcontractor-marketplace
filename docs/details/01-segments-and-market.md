# 01 — Segments and Market

## Two-sided customer definition

|  | Customer: project manager | Provider: contractor |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Project managers, site managers and procurement staff at established construction, renovation and fit-out firms | Builders, installers and trade crews — sole traders, small teams, mid-size subcontractors |
| Main need | Find a free, qualified crew quickly and compare quotes | Receive relevant jobs and show when the crew is free |
| Pain | Slow search, unreliable availability, no-shows | Irrelevant requests, missed opportunities, unpredictable schedule |
| Why they join | Saves time, shows real capacity, audit trail | Free listing, matched leads, calendar view |
| Buying power | Company budget, approval needed | Small business, price-sensitive |

The PM's job is to deliver projects on time, within budget and to the required quality. The intended outcome is not a longer list of contractors — it is a shorter time from identifying a need to securing a confirmed, suitable subcontractor.

The provider's job is to keep crews utilised. Free listings alone will not attract them; the platform must demonstrate that its requests are relevant and commercially worthwhile.

## Value proposition by segment

| Segment | Primary pain | Our value | Retention driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large construction companies | Time wasted on sourcing; unreliable commitments | Faster sourcing; verified availability; accountability records | Workflow integration; team collaboration; procurement history |
| Small fit-out companies | Limited networks; difficulty finding specialist trades | Access to broader provider pool; relevant matches | Repeat successful placements; time savings |
| Sole trader subcontractors | Unpredictable workflow; wasted time on irrelevant leads | Relevant requests; simple response process | Predictable access to work; capacity management tools |
| Small subcontracting teams | Capacity gaps; difficulty finding complementary trades | Ability to request other trades; visibility to larger clients | Network effects; subcontracting chain efficiency |

## Retention question

For PMs, value comes from repeatable sourcing, reliable availability, comparable responses and searchable booking history. For providers, from qualified demand, few irrelevant requests, simple capacity management and predictable access to work.

**Does the platform make the next subcontracting decision easier than the previous one?** If no, users leave after the first successful introduction.

## Why larger firms first

Advantages:

- More frequent subcontracting requirements; multiple concurrent projects.
- Established procurement processes and existing subcontractor networks they can invite.
- Stronger willingness to pay for measurable time savings.

Trade-offs: longer purchasing cycles, internal approval requirements, existing software. Interview larger firms first while testing the workflow with smaller, faster-moving companies.

## Ideal pilot customer

- Sources subcontractors regularly; manages several trades or projects.
- Experiences meaningful delays when finding available crews.
- Willing to share real, non-sensitive sourcing requests.
- Has a decision-maker who can give feedback and commit to testing over several weeks.

Prioritise companies with a recurring problem over companies that merely express interest.

## Pilot segment

Interior fit-out and renovation in Tallinn and Harju county. Proposed initial trades:

1. Electrical work
2. Plumbing
3. HVAC
4. Drywall and partition installation
5. Flooring
6. Painting and finishing

These trades are needed together on one project, creating repeated sourcing needs and natural multi-trade requests. The segment is a hypothesis: validate against actual demand, provider density, project frequency and willingness to adopt a new workflow.

## Market sizing framework

We do not yet have validated data to claim a market size. Build a bottom-up model from reachable customers and realistic usage — never a top-of-industry revenue figure.

| Metric | Data source | Validation method |
|---|---|---|
| Number of construction firms in Harju County | Estonian Business Register | Filter by EMTAK codes 41–43 |
| Number of fit-out specialists | Register of Economic Activities | Cross-reference with trade registrations |
| Average PM time spent on sourcing | Customer interviews | Ask for specific recent examples with time estimates |
| Subcontractor utilisation rates | Provider interviews | Ask about typical monthly capacity and gaps |
| Subcontracting requests per company per month | Interviews + pilot data | Count during concierge pilot |
| Providers contacted per request | Interviews + pilot data | Count during concierge pilot |
| Share of requests suited to online matching | Pilot data | Review request outcomes |
| Willingness to pay for workflow software | Pricing experiments | Test actual price points with design partners |
