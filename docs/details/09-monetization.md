# 09 — Monetization

Companion to [plan.md](../plan.md) §10.

## Stance

**Monetization is parked for later.** The only fixed rule so far:

> Listing and basic matching are free for providers, and the booking system interface is the paid part.

We do not charge providers to appear in search results before proving the platform creates meaningful demand. The model is a hypothesis to test during the pilot — not a decision to postpone indefinitely, and not one to force now.

## Revenue stream hypotheses

### Option A — Subscription for project managers

Charge construction companies for workflow features: request management, team collaboration, booking history, reporting, procurement coordination.

- **Pros:** predictable recurring revenue; clear link to operational efficiency; no incentive to sell irrelevant leads.
- **Open questions:** how frequently is the product used? Which functionality is worth paying for? Can we show measurable ROI?
- **Indicative pricing:** €200–500/month per company, depending on team size and features.

### Option B — Paid provider tools

Profiles and basic matching stay free; providers pay for capacity management, team scheduling, document management or admin tools.

- **Pros:** monetises value beyond lead access; may encourage repeat use.
- **Risk:** providers resist paying for software if they receive few relevant jobs.
- **Indicative pricing:** €50–100/month for premium tools.

### Option C — Transaction or booking fee

Fee on a successfully completed booking.

- **Pros:** revenue grows with marketplace activity.
- **Risks:** parties transact off-platform; construction contracts involve high values and complex payment arrangements; attribution, disputes and collection are difficult.
- **Indicative pricing:** 1–2% of booking value (requires booking-value tracking).

### Option D — Hybrid

Free basic access plus paid workflow features for those who get enough value. Probably the most flexible end state — validate willingness to pay before choosing it.

## Monetization experiments during the pilot

- Ask both sides which features they would pay for, and why.
- Where appropriate, test actual price points or request paid pilot commitments instead of relying on stated interest.
- Estimate: revenue per paying customer, sales and onboarding cost, ongoing support cost, gross margin, retention, revenue concentration, cost of maintaining provider quality and data.

The goal is to establish whether the marketplace can become a sustainable business — not merely whether people like using it.

## Disintermediation

Users will exchange contact details and may transact directly afterwards. We do not rely on artificial restrictions or on withholding contact information.

Retention is built from ongoing value:

- Easier sourcing for future projects.
- Reliable capacity records and booking history.
- Document management and team collaboration.
- Repeat provider relationships; faster responses and better matching.

**Retention should result from the platform being useful, not from making it difficult to leave.**
